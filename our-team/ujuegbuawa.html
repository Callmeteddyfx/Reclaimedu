Dr. Uju Egbuawa B.A, M.A(Phil), M.A (Gender),PDE, Ph.D.

Dr. Uju Egbuawa (she/her) is a philosopher, educator, and community-based researcher, and co-founder of ReclaimEdu. With over a decade of experience across higher education, academic leadership, and community-engaged research in Nigeria and Canada, her work centers on equity- driven education, critical inquiry, curriculum development and social transformation. Currently based on the unceded ancestral lands of the Lheidli T’enneh First Nation (Prince George, BC), Uju teaches philosophy at the College of New Caledonia and leads equity-oriented research and harm reduction programming at Positive Living North, an Indigenous-led HIV/AIDS service organization. Her work focuses on creating learning spaces that are inclusive, decolonial, trauma-informed, and grounded in lived experience. As a Black woman scholar and person of color, Uju brings a deeply relational and justice- centered approach to teaching and program design, with commitment to Indigenous communities, marginalized women, first-generation learners, and people navigating systemic exclusion. Her teaching span's moral philosophy, applied ethics, feminist philosophy, African philosophy, and contemporary social thought. Uju holds a PhD and M.A in Philosophy, M.A in Gender Studies, and professional certifications in education, planning, monitoring and evaluation, and mental health and substance use. Her scholarly and community work continues to shape her vision for education as a tool for dignity, critical consciousness, and reclaimed possibility.
Co-Founder’s Message: This consultancy was born from a simple truth: education should liberate, not exclude. We believe learning must challenge injustice, amplify marginalized voices, and create space for critical inquiry grounded in real lives. Together, we co-founded ReclaimEdu to challenge exclusionary systems of learning and to reclaim education as a tool for dignity, resistance, and possibility.
